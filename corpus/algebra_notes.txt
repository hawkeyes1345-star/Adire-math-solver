QUADRATIC EQUATIONS

To solve a quadratic equation of the form ax^2 + bx + c = 0:
First, try to factor it into (x - p)(x - q) = 0, where p and q are the roots.
The roots are found by setting each factor to zero.
If factoring is difficult, use the quadratic formula: x = (-b ± sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / (2a).
The discriminant D = b^2 - 4ac tells you the nature of the roots.

INTEGRATION

To integrate a polynomial, apply the power rule to each term:
The integral of x^n is x^(n+1)/(n+1), for n not equal to -1.
Always add the constant of integration C for indefinite integrals.

DIFFERENTIATION

The power rule for derivatives: the derivative of x^n is n*x^(n-1).
For products, use the product rule: (fg)' = f'g + fg'.
